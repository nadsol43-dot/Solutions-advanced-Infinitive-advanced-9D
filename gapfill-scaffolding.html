Scaffolding pack · advanced infinitive

The Chickasaw gap-fill — built up in 5 steps

Same task as the book, but the support is taken away slowly. Steps 1–3 are recognition (she chooses), step 4 is supported production, step 5 is the real thing.

1

Meet the six verbs

Goal: no unknown words

Before any grammar: make sure every verb in the box is a word she can explain. If she can't say what it means, the gap can't be solved.

Teacher: cover the middle column and ask her to explain each verb in her own words (L1 is fine). Only move on when all six are solid. 3 minutes.
2

Which of the four patterns is it?

Goal: recognition only

Every gap is one of the four patterns from the poster. Here she only has to name the pattern — no writing. This is the step that makes the rest possible.

1He realised he had a culture ______.
2He decided to learn more, ______ that fewer than 100 people spoke it.
3For ordinary speakers, a grammar book would be ___ technical ___.
4They weren't so interested in Chickasaw ______ it outside school.
5He made an app attractive ______ young people to want to use it.
6The very ___ person ___ only Chickasaw died in 2013.
Pattern key: only + to V disappointing result · noun / the last + to V replaces a relative clause · too / enough + to V the limit line · so + adj + as + to V degree → result
Teacher: the colour of each pattern matches the poster, so she can look at it while she works. There are two of each kind except "only" and "so…as".
3

One gap at a time — pick the phrase

Goal: choose, don't invent

The whole text is too much at once. Here each gap sits in its own sentence, with the sentence before it for context, a clue question, and three choices.

Before: His first son was born.

He then realised he had a culture ______, but was unable to do so.

Clue: "do so" refers back to a verb. What do you do with a culture? → give it to your children. A noun followed by an infinitive = noun + to V
a) to pass onb) passing onc) for pass on

Before: He decided to learn more.

He decided to learn more, ______ that fewer than 100 members of his tribe were fluent.

Clue: good news or bad news? He wanted to learn… and found out almost nobody speaks it. → only + to V = disappointing surprise.
a) only to discoverb) to discoverc) discovering

Before: He decided not to write a grammar book.

For ordinary speakers, that would be ___ technical ___.

Clue: two blanks, three words. The word technical is already there. Which limit word goes in front of an adjective? → too + adj + to V. Careful: one word before, two words after.
a) too … to useb) enough … to usec) so … that use

Before: He wanted to engage young people — a hard job.

They weren't so interested in Chickasaw ______ it outside school.

Clue: the word so is already in the sentence. When you see so + adjective, what word usually follows? → so + adj + as + to V.
a) as to studyb) enough to studyc) for studying

Before: He created a Chickasaw learning app.

He created an app that was attractive ______ young people to want to use it.

Clue: "attractive" is already the adjective, so the limit word comes after it. Which one means "reaching the level"? → adj + enough + to V. Note the second to: to want to use.
a) enough to motivateb) too to motivatec) so as motivate

Before: The app was a hit; the future looks brighter.

The very ___ person ___ only Chickasaw died in 2013.

Clue: two blanks again. After "the very", which word means "final"? Then: the last person who did what? → the last + to V. One word in the first blank, two in the second.
a) last … to speakb) first … to speakc) last … speaking
Teacher: she should say the whole finished sentence out loud after each choice — the ear catches what the eye misses. If she picks a distractor, ask "why not the other two?" before you explain.
4

The full text — with the clues still on

Goal: write, with support

Now the whole text, but every gap still carries its pattern label and a box for each of the three words. Count the boxes: if she has written two, one is missing.

Joshua Hinson of the Chickasaw Nation was not concerned with speaking the language of his people until his first son was born. He then realised he had a culture noun + to V, but was unable to do so with the few Chickasaw words he knew. He decided to learn more, only + to V that fewer than 100 members of his tribe were fluent in Chickasaw and they were all over 60. Hinson resolved to try to save his ancestral language. He decided not to write a grammar book because, for ordinary speakers, that would be too + adj + to V technical . Instead he tried to find a way to engage the young in his project, no mean feat considering they weren't so interested in Chickasaw so + adj + as + to V it outside school. He created a Chickasaw learning app that was attractive adj + enough + to V young people to want to use it. The app was an instant hit and now the future of Chickasaw looks brighter. The very the last + to V person only Chickasaw died in 2013, but thanks to Hinson, the language did not die with her.
Counting rule: every gap is exactly three words. Gaps 3 and 6 are split — the three words go in two different blanks (1 + 2). If a blank she filled sounds complete on its own, check the other half.
Support you can remove later: ① fold the page so the coloured labels are hidden · ② give her the first letters (t__ p___ o__) · ③ give her the six verbs on cards and let her arrange them physically before writing.

Answer key & teacher notes

Tear off / don't print this page for the student.

1 — to pass on · He had a culture to pass on. Noun + to V: the infinitive replaces “a culture that he could pass on”. The words “do so” two lines later are the give-away.
2 — only to discover · Disappointing result. Not plain to discover: the surprise is the point.
3 — too … to use · Split gap: too before “technical”, to use after it. Order: too + adj + to V.
4 — as to study · so is already in the text, so the gap is “as to + V”. Meaning: their interest never reached that level.
5 — enough to motivate · adjective + enough + to V. Two infinitives in a row is normal here: to motivate… to want… to use.
6 — last … to speak · Split gap: “the very last person to speak only Chickasaw”. The last + to V.
Why this task is hard (and what the steps do about it):
  • Six verbs, six gaps, but the verbs are not the answer — she must add helper words (only, too, enough, as, the last). Step 2 makes those helpers the focus.
  • Gaps 3 and 6 are split — the three words are not together. Step 4 says this out loud; the book doesn't.
  • Two infinitives in a row (gap 5: …to motivate young people to want to use it) sound wrong to a learner's ear. Say it aloud in step 3.
  • Hard vocabulary (ancestral, no mean feat, engage, fluent) is not tested, so pre-teach or gloss it — don't let it eat the grammar time.
Suggested 35-minute flow:
  • 0–5 min Step 1 — verbs, orally. Plus 2 minutes on the story: “a man trying to save a language”.
  • 5–12 min Step 2 — pattern naming only. She uses the poster as a reference.
  • 12–22 min Step 3 — one gap at a time, she says each finished sentence aloud.
  • 22–30 min Step 4 — full text with labels; you fade the support as she goes.
  • 30–35 min Step 5 — the book page, cold. It usually looks easy by now.
If she still stalls: go back to step 3, cover the wrong options, and give her the six verb cards plus helper-word cards (only · too · enough · as · the last · to) to arrange by hand.
